CHAPTER 01 — THE GLASS MERIDIAN


[voice:GREEN DRAGON] [emphasis]NAVIGATION UPDATE![/emphasis] Provisional world marker 1 of twenty-four: The Glass Meridian. The forward array shows a ring of transparent mountains. Our instruments identify refraction as the primary hazard. I am keeping the other three ships at a conservative distance until the crew understands the environment. No automated landing authorization has been issued.

[voice:BRIAN] Good. We are not rushing into The Glass Meridian because a map tells us it is next! I want the team thinking together, not waiting for a computer to hand us an answer. Rhea, establish the problem. Tirzah, track the human consequences. Mira, protect our margin for error. Everyone else, challenge the first explanation you hear. That is an order, and it is also how we keep each other alive.

[voice:RHEA] The immediate report is a missing beacon hidden in daylight. I have three independent data streams, and they do not agree. One shows an ordinary equipment fault, another shows a pattern in the timing, and the third is missing exactly where the problem becomes dangerous. [emphasis]That gap matters![/emphasis] If we pretend missing data is good data, we will write a beautiful report about a disaster we could have prevented.

[voice:TIRZAH] I am putting the people first. A distress report is not a puzzle somebody designed for our entertainment. If there are survivors at The Glass Meridian, they may be tired, frightened, or unable to explain what happened in the language our systems expect. We need a channel they can use without passing a technical examination. I will send a simple invitation: identify your location, tell us what you need, and know we are listening.

[voice:MIRA] Flight dynamics are stable for now, but refraction could turn a routine approach into a collision. I recommend a two-stage entry: first an unmanned sensor sweep, then a piloted maneuver only if the sweep confirms a safe corridor. The Green Dragon can hold the center line while Wickery watches the port arc, Viper 8 monitors the outer flank, and Ghost Ship keeps a silent reference marker behind us.

[voice:VESPER] Agreed, with one correction! Do not assume our familiar formation works in an unfamiliar field. The vessels have matching Federal-style silhouettes but different paint and independent telemetry. Green Dragon is green, Wickery orange, Ghost Ship black, and Viper 8 white. Their commanders are Brian Chapman, Eric Chapman, Mark Chapman, and David Stevenson respectively. We read their messages here; we do not invent their voices. I will confirm each response before shifting any ship.

[voice:SELENE] There is an anomaly in the long-range image: light arriving before its source. I checked the optics twice and found no evidence that the camera is simply broken. But an unusual reading is not proof of an extraordinary cause. I want a control image from another angle, a time stamp from another clock, and a physical measurement before anybody calls this a miracle or a trap. We can be curious and disciplined at the same time.

[voice:NYX] You have to admit, The Glass Meridian knows how to make an entrance! Still, I would rather be the person who asks an embarrassing question than the person who ignores a dangerous one. What if our first measurement changed the thing we are measuring? What if the signal is responding to us? And what if the silence between pulses is more informative than the pulses themselves? I am marking all three possibilities as questions, not answers.

[voice:RAZACI] The pattern resembles a feedback loop, not a simple broadcast. We send a test, the environment changes, and our instruments then report a different state. That can create a false sense of discovery. I propose a reference sequence: measure without transmitting, transmit a tiny controlled pulse, measure again, and compare the differences. We must preserve the raw readings so the whole team can reconstruct what we did if the interpretation proves wrong.

[voice:AURORA] I have created a shared evidence board. The left column contains observations, the center contains hypotheses, and the right contains actions we can reverse. Nothing is promoted to fact merely because it sounds compelling. I am also keeping the fleet archive separate from the game archive. The website can show the adventure without pretending its interactive display is a real navigation system. That distinction matters for the story and for the crew.

[voice:LESLEY] And while everyone builds a brilliant model, I will ask the practical question: how do we get everybody home? I am checking food, medical supplies, spare batteries, and the time needed to retreat. If align the prism arrays takes twice as long as expected, we need enough resources for the second half of the mission. Heroism is wonderful in a story, but a packed emergency kit has saved more arguments than a dramatic speech.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] First sweep complete. The environmental response confirms refraction is affecting our approach. I can estimate a safe boundary, but I cannot guarantee what lies beyond it. [emphasis]CAUTION![/emphasis] The unusual reading—light arriving before its source—has persisted through two independent scans. I am displaying uncertainty ranges rather than a single reassuring number. Commander, authorization is required before the next stage.

[voice:BRIAN] Authorization for observation only. No landing, no extraction attempt, and no forced connection to an unknown network. We have one chance to establish trust with this place. Tirzah, keep the rescue channel open. Selene, find a physical test that does not risk anyone. Rhea, tell me what result would prove your leading theory wrong. I want us to be willing to change our minds before the situation changes them for us.

[voice:RHEA] To falsify my leading theory, we should see the same timing pattern when our equipment is isolated. If the pattern disappears, the fault may be in our own instruments. I will shut down one receiver, leave a second untouched, and let a third run a calibration trace. The experiment is deliberately boring. That is the point! If we can explain the strange behavior with ordinary causes, we should not skip that explanation.

[voice:MIRA] The isolated receiver reports a different phase, and that gives us room to maneuver. I am drawing a narrow corridor around the highest-risk region. We can approach the outer boundary without crossing it. Wickery has acknowledged a holding position; Viper 8 has returned a flank-coverage marker; Ghost Ship has supplied a fixed reference point. None of those signals requests a change in command. The fleet is coordinated, and the crew remains responsible.

[voice:TIRZAH] The rescue channel has an answer. It is brief, and part of it is damaged, but it gives us a location and a request for time. Someone is still working on the other side. I will not turn that into a dramatic mystery when it is also a human voice asking us to be patient. We should send back the safest information we know: our position, our intention to help, and the fact that we will not make their situation worse by rushing.

[voice:SELENE] The control image is back. Light arriving before its source is real as an observed effect, but the cause is still open. I compared three wavelengths and found that one behaves differently. That suggests a property of the local environment rather than a trick of perspective. I am not naming a new law of physics on the strength of one afternoon. I am asking Razaci to compare the spectral shift with the pulse sequence.

[voice:RAZACI] Comparison complete! The sequence has a rhythm, but it is not a message in any language we have established. It looks like a system trying to keep itself stable while outside conditions change. Our first transmission may have nudged it. If we keep shouting at the system, we may push it farther from balance. I recommend we reduce power, wait for a full cycle, and then try the smallest correction capable of testing the idea.

[voice:NYX] So our grand discovery is that sometimes the smartest thing a spaceship can do is be quiet? I can work with that! There is a strange comfort in hearing the crew disagree without turning on each other. We have a real problem, not a competition for the cleverest sentence. I will log the questions we have not answered, especially the ones we are tempted to forget because they complicate the happy ending.

[voice:AURORA] The evidence board now shows a viable plan: align the prism arrays. The plan has a stop condition, a retreat path, and an independent verification step. If the environment responds outside our predicted range, the procedure ends automatically and we reassess. I have written the instructions in plain language alongside the technical notation. Everyone should be able to explain what we are doing before anyone presses a button.

[voice:LESLEY] I reviewed the plan for people rather than just machinery. We have a safety observer, a clear call to abort, and an extra supply reserve. I also asked the receiving station whether our proposed help is actually useful to them. They replied that it is, with one condition: do not disrupt the work they have already completed. That is a fair request. Rescue should not erase the judgment of the people we are rescuing.

[voice:BRIAN] Then we proceed together. [emphasis]ECHO TEAM, EXECUTE THE CONTROLLED PLAN![/emphasis] Mira holds the corridor. Rhea monitors the baseline. Selene watches the physical response. Razaci tracks feedback. Tirzah stays with the rescue channel. Vesper manages the formation. Lesley owns the safety stop. Aurora records every decision. Nyx, keep asking the question none of us wants to hear. Green Dragon, take us only as far as the evidence permits.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] Executing the first reversible step of align the prism arrays. Power remains below the agreed limit. The response is measurable, and the uncertainty interval is narrowing. The other three ships remain clear of the hazard. I have not interpreted a lack of alarm as proof of safety; all active monitors are reporting independently. [emphasis]FIRST STAGE COMPLETE![/emphasis] Awaiting team review before any additional change.

[voice:RHEA] The baseline held. That is our first solid result. The intervention changed the relevant measurement without disturbing the control receiver, which supports the proposed mechanism. It does not explain every strange detail, and it certainly does not make the missing data magically appear. But it gives us a reason to continue. I want a second test under the same conditions before we claim the problem is solved.

[voice:VESPER] Fleet review: all four ships are accounted for. Wickery has maintained its orange-hull position marker. Viper 8 is holding the white-hull outer line. Ghost Ship remains a black silhouette against the reference stars, and its navigation pulse is steady. Green Dragon has the central lane. No one has crossed the safety boundary. I am keeping the formation until we have a confirmed exit path, not just a successful first move.

[voice:MIRA] The second pass matches the first within tolerance. I can now fly the rescue corridor at reduced speed. I am keeping enough thrust in reserve to reverse the maneuver if the field changes. I have seen pilots mistake a clean approach for a guaranteed landing. We are not doing that. The corridor is a temporary opportunity, and every member of the crew has earned the right to say stop if the numbers change.

[voice:TIRZAH] The people at The Glass Meridian confirm the immediate danger has eased. They are not asking us to celebrate; they are asking for a careful handoff. I am sending our measurements and the limits of what we know. I will also tell them that the unusual reading, light arriving before its source, remains unexplained. Respect means leaving them with the truth, including the parts of the truth that are inconvenient.

[voice:SELENE] Before we leave, I want one last image of a ring of transparent mountains. Not for a trophy and not because it makes the mission look impressive. I want a record of the environment after our intervention. If we return, we will need to know what changed. I am tagging the image with instrument settings and a note that our interpretation is provisional. The next crew should be able to improve on our work, not merely admire it.

[voice:AURORA] The archive entry is complete. It includes the original report, the alternative hypotheses, the test sequence, the successful action, and the unresolved anomaly. It also links the crew conversation to the fleet log without mixing it into the separate books. The audiobook website can present this as one continuous episode while preserving the individual source chapters elsewhere. That way the listener gets a coherent journey, and the manuscripts keep their identities.

[voice:NYX] I have one final question for the record. If we had arrived here alone, would we have noticed the same pattern? Probably not. One person sees a machine, another sees a person, another sees the missing measurement. The trick is not that any one of us knows everything. The trick is that we keep listening long enough for the different pieces to become useful. That is a pretty good reason to keep a crew together.

[voice:RAZACI] The mission leaves us with a new clue: light arriving before its source. It does not tell us where the next world lies, but its timing resembles an interval recorded in the earlier archive. I will store the correlation without treating it as causation. If the pattern repeats across independent worlds, then we may have a larger structure to investigate. For now, the evidence supports only a connection worth testing. [emphasis]A CLUE, NOT A VERDICT![/emphasis]

[voice:LESLEY] Final safety count is complete. Supplies are accounted for, the rescue channel has our contact instructions, and nobody has been left waiting on a promise we cannot keep. I am closing the emergency kit and leaving the spare batteries where they belong. We can enjoy a little relief now. I know everyone wants to race to the next mystery, but I vote for water, food, and five quiet minutes first!

[voice:BRIAN] Granted. We did not win because we were the loudest ship in the sky. We made progress because we checked each other, listened to the people affected, and refused to confuse a guess with a fact. Green Dragon, log The Glass Meridian as a completed first-response mission with open scientific questions. Keep the fleet together. When the crew is ready, we will follow the next verified signal—and we will face it as the same eleven voices, one ECHO Team.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] [emphasis]CHAPTER 01 COMPLETE![/emphasis] First-response objective recorded. Fleet integrity confirmed. Unresolved observation retained for future analysis. The navigation archive now contains 1 provisional world entries. All systems are returning to cruise configuration. The next transmission will begin only when the crew authorizes it.
