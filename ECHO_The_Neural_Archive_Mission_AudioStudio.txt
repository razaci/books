[voice:VESPER]

CHAPTER TWO — THE LOCAL MACHINE

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
I traced the packet to the neural-network experiment on the local computer. It's a software model, not a biological brain. It learned patterns from project notes and generated an index.

[voice:RAZACI]
Can it alter the ship?

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
Not while the firewall holds. I'm physically separating the flight controller from the research system.

[voice:NYX]
Separation confirmed. Flight controls remain independent.

[voice:MIRA]
The index has four branches: personal history, the Ark, ECHO, and USS Dragon.

[voice:TIRZAH]
That sounds like the work we've been doing. How did it combine them?

[voice:BRIAN]
Years of notes, manuscripts, game designs, and experiments. I built some of the tools locally. Others are just ideas and fiction. The machine has access to copies of those files.

[voice:LESLEY]
Then its connections may be literary rather than technical. An index can link themes without discovering a hidden physical system.

[voice:SELENE]
But it's using those themes to build a route through the simulation.

[voice:RHEA]
Show me the route.

[voice:NYX]
Displaying. Twenty-four levels below an Ark-shaped structure. A shipyard at the bottom.

[voice:VESPER]
That wasn't in yesterday's map.

[voice:BRIAN]
It's from the Tabernacle project. We imagined the Ark as a computer architecture, with levels, terminals, and symbolic systems. It's a fictional design language.

[voice:MIRA]
Someone turned that language into a navigable environment.

[voice:RAZACI]
And embedded a distress beacon at the bottom.

[voice:TIRZAH]
We treat it as a simulated rescue until evidence says otherwise. We don't confuse an immersive environment with the real world.

[voice:BRIAN]
Understood. But if the simulation contains a crew in trouble, we still have a problem to solve. Prepare a controlled entry.

[voice:SELENE]

[voice:SELENE]

[voice:NYX]

[voice:TIRZAH]

[voice:MIRA]
Almond branches. Lamps. Curtains. Rooms arranged like nested memory structures.

[voice:LESLEY]
The imagery comes from the Ark and Tabernacle manuscripts. It could be a metaphor for attention, memory, and emotional processing, not a scientific model of the brain.

[voice:BRIAN]
That's how I used it. A way to teach complicated ideas through a story.

[voice:AURORA]

[voice:RAZACI]

[voice:MIRA]

[voice:BRIAN]
Do not play private material over the bridge.

[voice:VESPER]
Access locked. Only metadata is visible.

[voice:LESLEY]

[voice:BRIAN]
After the accident I had to rebuild ordinary skills. Wheelchair, then walker, then practice in a pool. I went back to school. GED, college, graduate study. None of that was simple.

[voice:TIRZAH]

[voice:BRIAN]
Exactly. We can use the facts I choose to share. We don't let an algorithm decide what my life means.

[voice:AURORA]

[voice:NYX]

[voice:BRIAN]

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]

[voice:SELENE]

[voice:RHEA]

[voice:MIRA]

[voice:TIRZAH]
