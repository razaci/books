USS DRAGON: THE TWENTY-FOUR WORLDS
BONUS EASTER EGG AUDIOBOOK
THE SWG TERMINAL TRANSMISSION

CHAPTER ONE — FIVE SHIPS AT THE EDGE OF THE MAP

[voice:BRIAN]
[emphasis]USS Dragon, bridge log.[/emphasis] We have reached the edge of the mapped corridor. Ahead of us is SWG Terminal, a station that shouldn't be broadcasting, in a sector that officially doesn't exist. Everybody, eyes up. I want answers before we make a move.

[voice:TIRZAH]
And I want you to stop calling a heavily armed, mysterious station a tourist attraction.

[voice:BRIAN]
I never said tourist attraction.

[voice:RHEA]
You said, and I quote, "Look at the lights! We should go there!"

[voice:SELENE]
[laughs] That's almost exactly what a tourist says.

[voice:MIRA]
For the record, those lights aren't ordinary station lamps. Their pulses are synchronized with the Dragon's navigation heartbeat.

[voice:RAZACI]
Confirmed. The station has recognized us, but not as a visiting ship. It recognizes a [emphasis]returning network.[/emphasis]

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
Captain, I have detected five allied transponder signatures in the outer approach. Their configurations are related, but their hull markings are distinct.

[voice:BRIAN]
Put them on the forward screen!

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
First contact: your orange Green Dragon. Second: Eric Chapman's yellow Wickery. Third: Mark Chapman's black Ghost Ship. Fourth: David Stevenson's white Viper 8. Fifth: Jon Chapman's red vessel. All five are accounted for.

[voice:VESPER]
Five ships. One rendezvous. No warning. Somebody planned a family reunion at the edge of the universe.

[voice:LESLEY]
Well, somebody had better have planned refreshments.

[voice:AURORA]
The formation is defensive. They're surrounding the approach corridor, not threatening us. Their combined shields are creating a passage through the station's interference.

[voice:NYX]
I don't like it. The Ghost Ship has almost no reflected signal. Mark could vanish in that shadow and nobody would know where he went.

[voice:BRIAN]
That's Mark's idea of making an entrance.

[voice:TIRZAH]
And Eric's yellow ship is impossible to miss.

[voice:RHEA]
Wickery is broadcasting a challenge beacon. Not a hostile one. More like he's saying, "Catch me if you can."

[voice:BRIAN]
[laughs] That sounds about right. But nobody races into an unknown station. Green Dragon, tell our ships to hold formation.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
Formation acknowledged. All five ships are maintaining position. Captain, the terminal is requesting an identity that is not stored in my current registry.

[voice:BRIAN]
Which identity?

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
The request reads: [emphasis]WHO REMEMBERS THE FIRST ARRIVAL?[/emphasis]

[voice:SELENE]
That doesn't sound like a docking request.

[voice:MIRA]
It's a memory challenge. The station isn't asking for a password. It's testing whether we know what happened here.

[voice:RAZACI]
There is an old data structure buried underneath the transmission. The syntax predates our current systems. I have seen fragments of this architecture before, in the archives connected to the civilization engine.

[voice:BRIAN]
The old system that worked before the flood?

[voice:RAZACI]
A related system, yes. But this is a separate installation. We shouldn't assume every mystery has the same answer.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Thank you. An actual reasonable statement from the mysterious computer network.

[voice:RAZACI]
I treasure these rare moments of approval.

[voice:LESLEY]
[laughs] Put that in the ship's history book.

[voice:VESPER]
Captain, the terminal is powering up external docking arms. None of them are aimed at us. They're aimed at the five smaller ships.

[voice:NYX]
Or at five ships it was expecting a very long time ago.

[voice:AURORA]
Wait. The docking arms are displaying different colors. Orange, yellow, black, white, and red.

[voice:BRIAN]
It knows our formation.

[voice:MIRA]
Or the formation was designed to answer the station. The sequence is being treated like a five-part key.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
New message. [emphasis]FIVE ARRIVE. ELEVEN SPEAK. ONE DOOR OPENS.[/emphasis]

[voice:RHEA]
Eleven speak? That's our bridge voice roster.

[voice:SELENE]
Brian, Tirzah, Rhea, Selene, Mira, Razaci, Green Dragon, Vesper, Lesley, Aurora, and Nyx. Eleven.

[voice:BRIAN]
Then nobody adds a voice without permission. We have our crew. We keep our crew.

[voice:TIRZAH]
And we don't let an ancient machine tell us who we are.

[voice:BRIAN]
Exactly. We tell it.

CHAPTER TWO — THE ELEVEN-VOICE KEY

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
The terminal has opened a narrow communications channel. It is requesting an ordered response from all eleven active bridge stations.

[voice:VESPER]
I can isolate the channel. If it tries to reach our flight controls, I'll cut it.

[voice:NYX]
I'll watch for anything hiding behind the signal. The last thing we need is a secret door with a secret trap behind it.

[voice:LESLEY]
I'm going to make a radical suggestion. We could ask it politely what it wants.

[voice:BRIAN]
That's why we keep you around, Lesley.

[voice:LESLEY]
I thought it was because I know where the good coffee is.

[voice:BRIAN]
Both can be true!

[voice:AURORA]
I'm reading a dormant chamber behind the terminal's outer wall. It's not a weapons bay. It's a relay theater, a place built to preserve and play back a recorded event.

[voice:MIRA]
A memory theater. That would explain the challenge.

[voice:RAZACI]
I have reconstructed the first portion of the interface. It doesn't want eleven passwords. It wants eleven independent acknowledgments. A consensus, not a command.

[voice:TIRZAH]
That's better. Nobody should be able to open it alone.

[voice:BRIAN]
All right, crew. One at a time. Tell the terminal what you bring to this mission. No speeches. Well, maybe short speeches.

[voice:RHEA]
[laughs] You're asking the wrong crew for short speeches.

[voice:BRIAN]
I'll start. I bring responsibility for every person on this bridge and every ship outside it. No one gets left behind.

[voice:TIRZAH]
I bring the courage to question the captain when he's about to do something spectacularly foolish.

[voice:BRIAN]
That happens less often than you imply.

[voice:TIRZAH]
The record will speak for itself.

[voice:RHEA]
I bring observation. If there's a clue hidden in the noise, I'll find it.

[voice:SELENE]
I bring patience. We don't have to solve every mystery by charging straight into it.

[voice:MIRA]
I bring analysis. A pattern is not proof until we test it.

[voice:RAZACI]
I bring memory and the willingness to correct my own conclusions when the evidence changes.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
I bring navigation, protection, and a promise to keep my crew informed of every decision I can explain.

[voice:VESPER]
I bring defense. Nothing crosses this bridge without my knowing.

[voice:LESLEY]
I bring care. Ships are machines. Crews are people. Remember the difference.

[voice:AURORA]
I bring the light that makes the unseen visible, and the discipline not to mistake every shadow for an enemy.

[voice:NYX]
And I bring the ability to find the hidden exits. Every place has one. Even a place that claims it doesn't.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
Eleven acknowledgments received. The terminal has accepted the sequence.

[voice:VESPER]
Something's moving!

[voice:NYX]
Outer wall opening. No weapons signature.

[voice:AURORA]
The chamber is projecting a star map. Twenty-four points of light, arranged around a central empty space.

[voice:SELENE]
The Twenty-Four Worlds.

[voice:MIRA]
Not exactly. These are twenty-four possible routes. Some lead to worlds. Some lead to archives. Some terminate at places that haven't been charted.

[voice:RAZACI]
The central empty space is labeled [emphasis]THE CHOICE NOT YET MADE.[/emphasis]

[voice:BRIAN]
So the terminal isn't telling us where to go. It's giving us a choice.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Now that sounds like a door worth opening.

CHAPTER THREE — THE TRANSMISSION THAT WASN'T LOST

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
Captain, the terminal has begun playback of an archived message. Its timestamp is incomplete. We cannot verify who recorded it.

[voice:BRIAN]
Put it on the bridge display, but keep it isolated.

[voice:MIRA]
The message is mostly visual. Five silhouettes stand before a star map. The recording has no recoverable names.

[voice:RHEA]
Look at the five symbols below them. They match the five colors outside.

[voice:SELENE]
Then somebody used a similar formation here before us.

[voice:RAZACI]
Possibly. But similarity isn't identity. We should not claim this proves our ships were here in the past.

[voice:NYX]
There's a second layer. Something tucked into the dark pixels between the stars.

[voice:AURORA]
I'll increase the contrast. There! A small line of text.

[voice:LESLEY]
Read it aloud, please.

[voice:AURORA]
[emphasis]THE DOOR OPENS ONLY WHEN NO ONE IS LEFT OUT.[/emphasis]

[voice:BRIAN]
Well, that settles it. Five ships. Eleven voices. Everybody gets a place in the formation.

[voice:TIRZAH]
And nobody gets to be erased just because the story becomes complicated.

[voice:VESPER]
Captain, the five ships are receiving the same star map. They're holding steady, waiting for your signal.

[voice:BRIAN]
Tell them we're not entering the chamber today. We're saving the map, recording the coordinates, and going home together.

[voice:RHEA]
You're turning down a mysterious door?

[voice:BRIAN]
I'm choosing a crew over a shortcut.

[voice:SELENE]
That may be the most surprising thing that's happened all day.

[voice:BRIAN]
Don't get used to it.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
Data transfer complete. Twenty-four route markers archived. All five allied vessels remain secure.

[voice:MIRA]
I found one final detail. The central point of the map isn't a destination. It's a blank space where a new route can be written.

[voice:RAZACI]
Then the archive is unfinished by design.

[voice:NYX]
A secret that doesn't force us to solve it. I almost respect that.

[voice:LESLEY]
Good. Then we can all get some rest before the next emergency.

[voice:VESPER]
You realize saying that aloud usually causes the next emergency.

[voice:LESLEY]
I withdraw the statement!

[voice:TIRZAH]
[laughs] Too late.

[voice:BRIAN]
Bridge crew, prepare departure. Orange Green Dragon on point. Yellow Wickery to port. Black Ghost Ship to starboard. White Viper 8 behind us. Red vessel in the rear guard. Nobody breaks formation.

[voice:AURORA]
The five colors are reflecting across the terminal windows. It looks like the station is saying goodbye.

[voice:SELENE]
Or waiting for us to return.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
Captain, one last signal has appeared. It is addressed to the crew of the USS Dragon.

[voice:BRIAN]
Read it.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
[emphasis]YOU FOUND THE EASTER EGG. THE REAL ADVENTURE CONTINUES TOGETHER.[/emphasis]

[voice:RHEA]
[laughs] That's it? That's the great cosmic secret?

[voice:MIRA]
For this transmission, apparently.

[voice:RAZACI]
Not every discovery must threaten civilization.

[voice:NYX]
Speak for yourself. I was hoping for at least one dramatic explosion.

[voice:VESPER]
Denied.

[voice:LESLEY]
Good!

[voice:TIRZAH]
Brian, are you smiling?

[voice:BRIAN]
Of course I'm smiling. We came to the edge of the map, found a mystery, kept the team together, and nobody blew up the station.

[voice:TIRZAH]
That's a personal record.

[voice:BRIAN]
[emphasis]USS Dragon, all ships—let's go home![/emphasis]

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
Course plotted. Formation confirmed. Eleven voices. Five ships. One crew.

END OF EASTER EGG CHAPTER
