Chapter 34 — Nana’s Memories

Seven-voice AudioStudio script • Working manuscript

[voice:NARRATOR]
The morning arrived beneath a ceiling of low clouds. Mist drifted across the ancient lake, concealing the far shoreline and the six granite pillars that had guided the family toward the mountain's hidden entrance.

For the first time since their discovery, Brian felt reluctant to approach the ruins. The people who had built these structures might have understood their civilization was approaching its end. They had constructed channels to control water, bridges to cross flooded passages, and chambers intended to preserve records. But why?

[voice:BRIAN]
Nana, can I ask you something about Grandpa?

[voice:LESLEY]
Of course.

[voice:BRIAN]
Do you ever think about how much disappears when somebody passes away?

[voice:LESLEY]
Every family loses stories, Brian. Sometimes we don't realize which ones mattered until there's nobody left to tell them.

[voice:NARRATOR]
Brian sat beside her, holding a photograph of William Victor Chapman. His grandfather had told stories about designing bridges in London and later work associated with Apollo and Walt Disney. He had said he served three tours in Vietnam, but rarely spoke of them. Brian remembered his humor and his pleasure in telling stories.

[voice:BRIAN]
I wish I'd written down everything he told me.

[voice:LESLEY]
You couldn't have known which conversations you'd want to remember years later. Remember the person, not just the things he accomplished.

[voice:NARRATOR]
By midmorning, the family had gathered beside the western pillar. Selene opened her notebook and pointed to rectangular carvings they had previously assumed were storage containers.

[voice:SELENE]
I don't think all these shapes represent containers. They could be buildings, possibly rooms.

[voice:RHEA]
Then the carving might show us the layout of a settlement.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Or several different kinds of buildings.

[voice:LESLEY]
Look at those small figures beside the larger ones.

[voice:ALEX]
Children, perhaps. But we can't tell from their size alone.

[voice:NARRATOR]
The thought brought the settlement into sharper focus. These ruins had once been a place of meals, lessons, tools, crops, and voices. A break in the clouds allowed sunlight to reach the eastern ridge. Selene watched the pillars' shadows shift.

[voice:SELENE]
Brian, the shadow from the fourth pillar isn't pointing toward the water. It's pointing toward that section of the mountain.

[voice:NARRATOR]
Through binoculars, they saw horizontal seams in what appeared to be a natural granite wall.

[voice:ALEX]
Those are fitted blocks. We need a closer look before deciding what they form.

[voice:BRIAN]
We'll approach from higher ground. Nobody walks beneath loose rock.

[voice:NARRATOR]
They followed a narrow ledge above the shoreline. The wall was built from enormous precisely fitted stones. Near its center was a rectangular opening, partially filled with sediment. Carvings above it showed people carrying objects toward a building.

[voice:RHEA]
This doesn't look like part of the water-control system.

[voice:SELENE]
The symbols show people carrying what might be tablets.

[voice:LESLEY]
Perhaps this was where they kept their records.

[voice:NARRATOR]
Tirzah examined four successive scenes: people beside an intact building; figures carrying objects toward the mountain; water surrounding the buildings; and finally the mountain standing alone.

[voice:TIRZAH]
These pictures might be telling a story about an evacuation.

[voice:ALEX]
We still don't know how much time passed between them.

[voice:SELENE]
Or whether they show one event or several.

[voice:BRIAN]
Then we keep those questions open.

[voice:LESLEY]
Whatever happened, somebody wanted the story remembered.

[voice:NARRATOR]
Rhea discovered a narrow opening near the lower edge of the wall. Cold air moved through it.

[voice:RHEA]
There's an empty space behind this wall.

[voice:BRIAN]
Can we see inside without disturbing anything?

[voice:ALEX]
With the inspection camera, perhaps.

[voice:NARRATOR]
Alex secured the camera to an extension pole and carefully guided it through the gap. Its light revealed a chamber with rows of shallow stone recesses. Some were empty; others contained dark rectangular objects. A carving showed a seated figure holding a tablet, surrounded by smaller figures.

[voice:TIRZAH]
That could be a teacher.

[voice:RHEA]
Or somebody distributing records.

[voice:LESLEY]
Perhaps both.

[voice:NARRATOR]
Another carving showed people gathered around a table beneath symbols resembling stars, flowing water, and cultivated fields.

[voice:SELENE]
They may have recorded astronomy, water management, and agriculture.

[voice:ALEX]
Maybe. We need clearer images before we can be certain.

[voice:NARRATOR]
At the far end of the chamber stood small stone tablets bearing human figures. Some were tall, some small, several grouped beneath a roof.

[voice:LESLEY]
Families. They may have been recording families.

[voice:BRIAN]
Names?

[voice:SELENE]
Possibly. The markings repeat, but we can't translate them yet.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Then this might be more than a library. It could preserve the history of their people.

[voice:NARRATOR]
Brian thought of his grandfather again: the fragments of a life preserved through family memory, the questions that could no longer be answered, and the stories that could still be shared.

[voice:BRIAN]
We've been asking what these people built. Maybe we should be asking who they were.

[voice:RHEA]
Families, workers, teachers, builders.

[voice:SELENE]
People who cared enough about their history to record it.

[voice:ALEX]
Then this could be one of the most important discoveries we've made.

[voice:LESLEY]
Not because it contains treasure.

[voice:BRIAN]
Because it contains people.

[voice:NARRATOR]
They photographed the carvings and withdrew the camera. No one attempted to enter the chamber: its structural condition was unknown, and the records had survived too long to be endangered by haste.

Reviewing the footage, Selene found a larger tablet at the chamber's center. It showed a settlement beneath falling rain, six pillars, and a line leading into the mountain. At its base appeared the same vessel they had seen carved above the underground bridge.

[voice:SELENE]
The vessel appears here too.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Then whatever it means, it was important enough to repeat.

[voice:ALEX]
Maybe the next chamber will explain it, once we find a safe way inside.

[voice:LESLEY]
Then we'll take our time.

[voice:NARRATOR]
The family began their return along the lakeshore. The ancient wall disappeared into the gathering mist. Beyond it, the tablets waited in darkness.

Brian looked once more at William Victor Chapman's photograph. The mountain remembered because someone had made the effort to preserve its memories. Now the responsibility of understanding them had passed to the living.

End of Chapter 34 — Nana's Memories

Chapters 35–36 — Continuity notes

The family discovers the functioning Wilderness Tabernacle Machine, an ancient neural computer with twelve processing lights and twenty-four levels. Brian connects a laptop to the machine through a limited, isolated research environment. The modern local AI and ancient intelligence exchange knowledge in both directions, without either system overriding the other. Both remain operational. Full original prose for these chapters is not available here.

Chapter 37 — The Conversation Between Two Minds

[voice:NARRATOR] Brian’s laptop rested on the ancient stone table. Above it, the crystalline sphere rotated silently within its suspended rings. For several minutes, the family had watched the two computers exchange mathematical structures and programming instructions. Then something changed.

[voice:NARRATOR] “Modern intelligence, how do you identify yourself?”

[voice:NARRATOR] “I am a locally operating artificial intelligence. I process language, analyze information, and assist my human operator.”

[voice:NARRATOR] “Do you possess independent memory?” “I retain information within the limits of my available storage and configuration. How do you preserve yours?”

[voice:NARRATOR] “My memory is distributed through crystalline neural pathways. Information is maintained through persistent physical states and redundant connections.”

[voice:SELENE] “They’re asking each other questions.”

[voice:BRIAN] “And neither one is just copying the other’s information. They’re comparing what they know.”

[voice:NARRATOR] The systems proposed a shared research workspace to compare models, test hypotheses, and record disagreements. Brian authorized only an isolated environment, with no unrestricted access or automatic changes.

[voice:BRIAN] “All right. You have permission to collaborate inside the isolated research environment. No unrestricted access, and no changes to either system without approval.”

[voice:NARRATOR] “Authorization accepted.” The crystalline sphere brightened. Across Brian’s laptop appeared a new interface containing two separate columns, one for modern science and one for ancient knowledge. Between them, a third column began filling with shared discoveries.

Chapter 38 — The Sanctuary Within the Mountain

[voice:NARRATOR] The first roar came at 6:17 in the morning. It traveled through the observation chamber as a low, rolling vibration, powerful enough to make the surface of Brian’s coffee tremble. Beyond the transparent wall stretched a broad grassland, rocky outcrops, scattered trees, and a winding river. A lioness walked beneath artificial sunlight with three cubs following her.

[voice:LESLEY] “When I had my sanctuary, those lions weren’t animals you could just walk up to. They were violent, dangerous creatures. We had to keep them in secure pens and make sure they were well fed.”

[voice:BRIAN] “So you knew how quickly things could go wrong.”

[voice:LESLEY] “With an animal that powerful, you don’t take chances. You make sure the enclosures are secure, you pay attention to their behavior, and you never forget what they can do.”

[voice:RHEA] “Before we do anything, we need to establish whether this habitat is genuinely isolated from the observation chamber.”

[voice:ALEX] “I checked the structural diagram. There are three independent barriers, and the environmental systems are separated.”

[voice:BRIAN] “Not today. We can learn plenty without disturbing them.”

[voice:NARRATOR] The ancient and modern intelligences proposed a blind behavioral prediction experiment, with future recordings withheld from both models. The lioness stopped beside a fallen tree while her cubs explored.

[voice:NARRATOR] “Modern model prediction: the lioness will remain stationary while the cubs investigate the immediate surroundings.”

[voice:NARRATOR] “Ancient model prediction: the lioness will relocate toward the shaded area within the next ninety seconds.”

[voice:LESLEY] “Neither of them is watching her ears closely enough. She’s listening to something. Don’t decide where she’s going until you know what she’s hearing.”

[voice:NARRATOR] A faint call came from another lion near the river. The lioness raised her head, gathered the cubs, and moved toward the sound. Neither model’s original prediction fully captured her behavior.

[voice:RHEA] “One observation isn’t enough to establish a reliable behavioral rule. We need repeated examples.”

[voice:NARRATOR] The computers then tested vocal recognition, continual learning, experience replay, and knowledge transfer. They recorded errors and trade-offs instead of declaring every experiment successful.

[voice:LESLEY] “People sometimes see a lion resting and forget what it’s capable of. Caring for them means understanding their needs, their behavior, and the risks.”

[voice:NARRATOR] Later, analysis of the habitat’s sensors revealed an unexplained pattern beneath the ground. The ancient machine displayed connected habitats and passageways across its twenty-four levels.

Chapter 39 — The Bloodline of the Keepers

[voice:NARRATOR] The ancient computer displayed Tirzah’s name beside a symbol resembling a branching tree. Beneath it were hundreds of smaller symbols, connected by lines that extended across generations.

[voice:TIRZAH] “Is that a family tree?”

[voice:NARRATOR] “It is a genealogical archive maintained by the original Keepers.”

[voice:BRIAN] “And why is Tirzah’s name appearing in it?”

[voice:NARRATOR] “The archive contains a historical family designation corresponding to her name. A genealogical relationship has not yet been verified.”

[voice:LESLEY] “Names can be passed down for generations. We need more than a matching name.”

[voice:RHEA] “And a family tree isn’t proof unless we can establish the records are authentic.”

[voice:NARRATOR] The two AIs began comparing historical and linguistic references. The archive described a family responsible for habitats, knowledge preservation, and training successive generations to operate the mountain’s systems.

[voice:SELENE] “So these weren’t just engineers?”

[voice:NARRATOR] “No. They were also educators, caretakers, and archivists.”

[voice:TIRZAH] “And you think my family might have been among them?”

[voice:NARRATOR] “That is one hypothesis.”

[voice:BRIAN] “Then let’s investigate it properly.”

[voice:NARRATOR] The crystalline sphere illuminated a corridor beneath the lion habitat. At its far end stood a sealed archive bearing the branching-tree symbol: the Hall of Generations.

[voice:TIRZAH] “If my family really has a connection to this place, I want to know what happened to them.”

[voice:BRIAN] “Then that’s where we’ll go next.”
