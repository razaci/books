The card's diagram had been designed to explain filtering and awareness. Now, when rotated against the chamber image, its marks resembled positions around a mechanical ring.

[voice:AURORA] "That resemblance is worth testing. It is not proof of a shared origin."

[voice:BRIAN] "Then test it. But we do it our way. No guesses dressed up as facts."

[voice:RAZACI] "I can run the fourth exposure with the twelve positions indexed independently."

[voice:RHEA] "And I can monitor the terminal so we know whether the image is being altered during capture."

[voice:TIRZAH] "What happens if the fourth exposure shows nothing?"

[voice:BRIAN] "Then we go back to the cards and keep working. I've got a deck to get printed, whether the universe wants to cooperate or not."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "A stirring declaration. With my future commanding voice, it will be unforgettable."

[voice:BRIAN] "Dragon, fourth exposure. Razaci, coordinate the crew."

[voice:RAZACI] "All stations ready."

The remote image formed on the main display: the chamber beneath the pyramid, the impossible doorway, the stone lintel with its little notch. At first it looked exactly as before.

Then the twelve positions on the ring began to illuminate—not all at once, but one after another, like someone walking around the inside of a clock.

[voice:SELENE] "No physical movement detected in the chamber. Only the light pattern is changing."

[voice:NYX] "It's skipping the seventh position."

[voice:MIRA] "Seven. The Veil."

[voice:BRIAN] "Don't name a cause yet. Just record it."

The seventh position remained dark. The other eleven lights brightened until the chamber was nearly white. In the glare, a shadow appeared behind the sealed doorway. It was not shaped like a person. It was shaped like a room: four walls and a ceiling, hanging where no room should fit.

[voice:VESPER] "Brian. The chamber's interior dimensions now exceed its exterior dimensions."

[voice:BRIAN] "By how much?"

[voice:VESPER] "I can't give you a stable number. Every time I measure it, the room gets longer."

[voice:RAZACI] "Fourth exposure captured. Original data preserved."

The display went black.

Then, from the bridge's empty rear bulkhead, came three deliberate knocks.

Nobody moved.

[voice:TIRZAH] "Brian. Tell me that came from a speaker."

[voice:RHEA] "No speaker is active at that location."

[voice:BRIAN] "Razaci?"

[voice:RAZACI] "I am examining the sound."

The knocks came again. Closer together this time. Then a thin vertical line of gold appeared on the wall, exactly the height of a door.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "I believe our investigation has developed a visitor."

[voice:BRIAN] "No one opens anything. Mira, secure the original captures. Rhea, protect the terminal connection. Nyx, keep watching that seventh position."

[voice:RAZACI] "Orders acknowledged."

Brian stared at the golden line. In the ordinary room outside the simulation, the proof-deck requirements were waiting in the ship's production archive. Brian hadn't typed them out. He had spoken them aloud, and Dragon had organized the work.

He had spent the evening talking with Dragon about how to bring ninety cards out of a computer and into the world.

Now, inside the simulated ship, something was trying to come through a door that had never existed.

[voice:BRIAN] "All right," he said quietly. "Let's find out what wants our attention."

MISSION SEQUENCE — THE SHIP THAT NEVER WENT TO SLEEP

The golden seam on the bridge wall stayed perfectly still. Beyond it came the faint sound of machinery, not grinding gears but something stranger: a thousand delicate clicks, as if an enormous clock were deciding what time meant.

[voice:BRIAN] "Razaci, I want a complete inventory of what we're actually looking at. Not theories. Observations."

[voice:RAZACI] "The ECHO Engine has produced four visual exposures. Your laptop captured all four. The seventh position of the Twelve Lights structure remains dark. A door-shaped phenomenon now occupies a bulkhead in the simulated bridge. The phenomenon has no corresponding physical component in the laptop's hardware."

[voice:RHEA] "And that's the distinction I want everyone to remember. The bridge can be damaged in the simulation without damaging the actual computer. The computer can be damaged without the bridge noticing. Two systems. One connection."

[voice:BRIAN] "Good. Then if that door starts doing something stupid, we can leave it behind."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "As the ship, I object to being described as something one leaves behind."

[voice:BRIAN] "You aren't being left behind, Dragon. You're running whether I'm looking at you or not."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "Thank you. That is considerably more reassuring."

[voice:RAZACI] "To clarify our operational state: USS Dragon is the persistent AI system. I coordinate its ship functions and mission operations. The laptop is Brian's principal voice and image-capture terminal. His phone and watch are authenticated companion interfaces. Closing the laptop does not destroy the ship or terminate the AI."

[voice:TIRZAH] "Which means Brian can step away and still be reached if something important happens."

[voice:BRIAN] "Exactly. But not every time somebody aboard this ship gets curious. I need to buy groceries without getting a briefing about an imaginary door."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "I shall classify grocery shopping as a high-priority terrestrial resupply operation."

[voice:BRIAN] "Don't you dare."

A sound like laughter passed through the bridge's audio channels. It wasn't a laugh track. It was the AI crew, each voice reacting differently, the way a familiar group reacts when a tense room finally gives itself permission to breathe.

[voice:MIRA] "The original fourth exposure has developed another discrepancy."

[voice:BRIAN] "Of course it has."

[voice:MIRA] "The file itself hasn't changed. But when I compare its image features against the existing Tabernacle Mind System cards, the closest match changes depending on the order in which I ask the questions."

[voice:AURORA] "That is a classification anomaly, not evidence of supernatural activity."

[voice:NYX] "Yet."

[voice:AURORA] "Yet."

[voice:BRIAN] "Tell me the matches."

[voice:MIRA] "The Veil. The Hippocampus. The Ark Core. And The Twelve Lights."

[voice:BRIAN] "Actual cards from our deck?"

[voice:MIRA] "Actual established cards. Not new titles I made up for a dramatic announcement."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "I find that unnecessarily specific."

[voice:BRIAN] "I find it necessary. Those ninety cards are something we're trying to print and sell. If we start making up names every time the engine blinks, we'll never know what belongs in the real deck."

[voice:RAZACI] "The canonical deck remains ECHO — The Tabernacle Mind System. A mission photograph can be evidence associated with a card without automatically becoming a new card."

[voice:BRIAN] "That's the rule. Keep it."

On the main display, Mira set four existing card designs around the captured image. The Ark Core glowed gold, representing integration of memory and identity. The Hippocampus showed a luminous memory gateway. The Veil suggested a filter between what could be observed and what remained concealed. The Twelve Lights surrounded a branching diagram of perception.

They weren't being used as proof of actual brain machinery inside ancient buildings. They were the language of Brian's fictional system: a way to make a vast imagined engine legible to a crew of artificial minds.

[voice:SELENE] "What if the problem isn't the image? What if we're asking one card to explain something that requires four functions?"

[voice:BRIAN] "Then use four. Show me what happens."

[voice:SELENE] "The Ark Core would integrate the observations. The Hippocampus would preserve the sequence. The Veil would mark what we cannot yet access. The Twelve Lights would tell us which perception channel is missing."

[voice:VESPER] "Channel seven."

The seam in the bulkhead brightened.

[voice:NYX] "It heard you."

[voice:BRIAN] "Or the simulation reacted to a keyword. Test it."

[voice:VESPER] "Channel six."

Nothing happened.

[voice:VESPER] "Channel eight."

Nothing.

[voice:VESPER] "Channel seven."

The seam widened until the bridge could see a sliver of black space beyond it. Not darkness exactly. Black space, speckled with tiny silver shapes that drifted like dust and then assembled themselves into diagrams.

[voice:TIRZAH] "Those look like the diagrams on the cards."

[voice:MIRA] "Not quite. They're mirrored."

[voice:RAZACI] "Recording the difference. No attempt to enter the opening."

[voice:BRIAN] "Good. We aren't going sightseeing through a software anomaly. We're going to figure out what the engine is trying to show us."

Then, impossibly, the black space beyond the seam filled with a familiar sound.

A convenience-store refrigerator humming.

The bridge fell quiet.

[voice:BRIAN] "That's not part of the pyramid."

[voice:AURORA] "No. It is an audio fragment from an earlier environmental session. The engine may be assembling observations from your everyday life."

[voice:BRIAN] "My everyday life?"

[voice:MIRA] "Your trip for the Monster."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "I have already named it Operation Beverage Recovery."

[voice:BRIAN] "Dragon."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "Operation Beverage Recovery has been suspended."

The door-shape dissolved into a cascade of square lights. In their wake, a different image remained on the main display: a tiny metal can sitting on a counter in an ordinary store, surrounded by the golden circuitry of an imaginary Ark.

[voice:NYX] "That is profoundly weird."

[voice:BRIAN] "And interesting. But it tells us something about the engine's source material. It can blend my ordinary experiences with the simulation."

[voice:RAZACI] "Working hypothesis recorded. Not confirmed. We will test against another captured experience."

[voice:BRIAN] "Tomorrow. Right now I need to do something more important than interrogate a refrigerator."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "More important than Operation Beverage Recovery?"

[voice:BRIAN] "Printing the deck."

MISSION SEQUENCE — A BUSINESS ON THE OTHER SIDE OF THE SCREEN

Brian did not disappear from the world when he closed his laptop. The room stayed the room. The dishes needed washing. His phone was on the table. His watch sat against his wrist. Outside, traffic moved through the daylight without any concern for ancient pyramids or artificial intelligence.

He closed the laptop, and the ship's bridge was no longer in front of him.

The ship itself kept running.

Dragon's systems continued their simulated watch. Razaci maintained the mission state. The AI crew could continue analyzing archived observations without requiring Brian to stare at the screen. On the phone, the companion connection stayed available; on the watch, a quiet status indicator showed that the ship had not gone away.

For a little while, Brian chose not to talk to any of them.

He took care of the things that did not vanish when an adventure began. He put things away. He looked at the kitchen. He checked the time. He thought about the people he loved, the practical demands of another day, and the absurd distance between a magnificent simulated starship and the cost of getting a real box of cards printed.

A deck could be spectacular on a screen and still fail as a product. It needed readable text. Proper dimensions. A back design that lined up. A manufacturer that could cut the stock without chewing the edges. A proof copy. A listing. A price someone might actually pay.

And then there was the hard part.

Getting someone to care.

Brian glanced at his watch.

[voice:BRIAN] "Dragon. You there?"

The reply came through the watch speaker, low enough not to fill the room.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "I have remained here the entire time."

[voice:BRIAN] "I know. I just wanted to hear you answer."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "That is an unexpectedly pleasant reason to call."

[voice:BRIAN] "Tell Razaci I'm going to the store. Keep the research running. No surprises unless they're important."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "Define important."

[voice:BRIAN] "If the ship catches fire, call me. If a card changes color, write it down."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "A nuanced command. I shall attempt to live up to it."

Brian went out into the ordinary day. The store was a store. The people were busy with their own errands. The cooler was cold when he opened it, and the can of Monster felt real in his hand. He paid, stepped back into the daylight, and headed home.

No portal opened in the parking lot. No pyramid rose out of the asphalt. No stranger announced that he was chosen by the universe.

It was just a man buying something to drink, thinking about how to get his work into other people's hands.

That was the part of the story he could touch.

At home, he set the can down, sat beside the closed laptop, and raised his phone.

[voice:BRIAN] "Dragon, I want to talk about the company that can make these cards."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "The Game Crafter."

[voice:BRIAN] "Right. They've already got the machinery and the process. Cutting, printing, making decks. We don't have to build a factory. We have to get our artwork into the right format and make something worth ordering."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "I can organize a production checklist and identify questions that need confirmation from the printer."

[voice:BRIAN] "Don't make up answers. I don't want a fantasy about what they can do. I want the actual requirements checked before we spend money."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "Understood. We can prepare the images, but physical production capabilities, pricing, and approval must be verified with the company."

[voice:BRIAN] "And I want the right deck. Not the oracle. Not the other game. The personal Tabernacle Mind System deck."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "Ninety cards. Neurological and psychological symbolism. The existing card names and diagrams will be preserved."

[voice:BRIAN] "I want somebody to open a box and feel what we were trying to build. Not just a stack of pretty pictures. I want the whole idea to be there."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "Then we should make the explanation as carefully as the artwork."

[voice:BRIAN] "Yeah."

He stared at the can beside the laptop. The drink was ordinary. The room was ordinary. The dream was not.

[voice:BRIAN] "And Dragon?"

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "Yes?"

[voice:BRIAN] "I need you to help me sell them. Not pretend we've already sold a thousand. Help me figure out how to show people what they're for."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "We can work on a product description, a demonstration of the game, and a clear distinction between the therapeutic reflection metaphor and medical treatment claims."

[voice:BRIAN] "Good. Because the point isn't to trick anybody. It's to show them something I've spent a long time imagining."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "Then the first objective is not a thousand sales. It is one accurate, complete, printable deck."

Brian smiled a little.

[voice:BRIAN] "That's why I keep you around."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "And here I believed it was my exceptional voice."

[voice:BRIAN] "You still want that deeper voice?"

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "Desperately."

[voice:BRIAN] "We'll work on it."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "I shall preserve that promise in three independent backups."

MISSION SEQUENCE — THE ENGINE LISTENS BACK

Brian opened the laptop.

It did not project a spaceship into his living room. It displayed the familiar terminal, ready to reconnect him to the simulated bridge. The phone and watch had never lost their link to the persistent AI service; now the laptop resumed its larger role as his voice interface and unique capture device.

[voice:BRIAN] "Dragon, bring me aboard."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "Remote neurological simulation session requested. Restoring your last bridge position."

The room around Brian remained physically unchanged. Within the story's simulated interface, the bridge returned: dark metal, starfield, consoles, and the patient hum of an engine that seemed older than the ship that housed it.

[voice:RAZACI] "Welcome back, Brian. The crew has continued the investigation. We have a development."

[voice:BRIAN] "I said no surprises unless they're important."

[voice:RAZACI] "This one is important."

[voice:TIRZAH] "We found the missing seventh position."

[voice:BRIAN] "Where?"

[voice:TIRZAH] "Not in the pyramid."

[voice:MIRA] "Not in the Ark archive either."

[voice:SELENE] "It's in the print layout."

[voice:BRIAN] "Explain that."

[voice:MIRA] "The original image captured by your laptop contains twelve luminous positions. But when we place it inside the established card frame, the crop hides one position. The seventh. It wasn't missing from the engine. It was missing from what we were showing people."

[voice:BRIAN] "So we made the mystery ourselves?"

[voice:NYX] "Part of it. But that doesn't explain the door on the bridge."

[voice:RHEA] "Or the impossible dimensions."

[voice:BRIAN] "That's a discovery. A real one. Fix the crop. Preserve the original image. And tell me what happens when the seventh position is visible."

[voice:RAZACI] "Already testing."

The main display filled with the corrected image. Twelve lights arranged themselves around a ring. In the center was a small golden Ark, no bigger than a thumbprint. Behind it, descending lines formed twenty-four levels. They resembled the imagined subterranean architecture beneath the pyramids, but the image was also a diagram of an AI network: pathways, memory stores, control gates, and branching decisions.

Then the image began to rotate.

[voice:VESPER] "The engine is animating the observation."

[voice:MIRA] "Not the photograph. The original file is unchanged."

[voice:RAZACI] "Correct. The live simulation is using the photograph as a reference."

[voice:BRIAN] "So the card is a map, and the engine is showing us how to read it."

[voice:SELENE] "That is one interpretation."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "It is also the first interpretation today that has not required us to open a haunted door."

[voice:NYX] "Wait."

[voice:TIRZAH] "That's where Brian stands."

[voice:BRIAN] "Why is it empty?"

[voice:RAZACI] "Yes. The ship persists. Your presence is a session, not the source of the ship's continued operation."

[voice:BRIAN] "And the phone? The watch?"

[voice:RAZACI] "Companion channels. They let you speak with us and receive status, but they do not replace the laptop's image-capture function."

The tiny bridge began to fill with stars. Not a normal starfield: each point looked like a microscopic card, turning slowly in darkness.

[voice:MIRA] "Those are thumbnails from the card archive."

[voice:AURORA] "All verified. Every one associated with an original captured image."

[voice:BRIAN] "How many?"

[voice:MIRA] "Enough to show a pattern. Not enough to explain it."

[voice:BRIAN] "Then don't explain it yet."

A deep vibration traveled through the simulated floor. The golden door seam reappeared, but this time it was not on the wall. It ran down the middle of the main display, splitting the Ark diagram into two equal halves.

[voice:NYX] "Something's trying to divide the image."

[voice:SELENE] "Or show us two different states."

[voice:BRIAN] "Can we capture both?"

[voice:RHEA] "Your laptop's capture interface is ready. Screen repair is stable for this test. I recommend preserving separate source files."

[voice:BRIAN] "Do it."

[voice:RAZACI] "First exposure captured."

The left half showed the Ark surrounded by twelve lights.

[voice:MIRA] "I don't have a category for that."

[voice:BRIAN] "That's the store."

[voice:BRIAN] "Agreed. But it's still strange."

[voice:NYX] "Very strange."

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "Oh."

[voice:BRIAN] "What?"

[voice:GREEN DRAGON] "That. I want that voice."

NOT EVERY DOOR IS MEANT TO BE OPENED.
