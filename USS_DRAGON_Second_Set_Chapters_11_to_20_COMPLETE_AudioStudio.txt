USS DRAGON — BOOK FOUR
THE CREW THAT HELD THE LINE

CHAPTER ONE — THE SILENT STATION

[voice:NARRATOR]
The USS Dragon crossed a region of dark space where the stars appeared strangely distant. Ahead, a station hung without running lights. Its docking ring was intact. Its windows were black.

[voice:BRIAN]
Green Dragon, bring us alongside, but don't dock yet. I want to know why that station is sitting out here without a beacon.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
Understood, Brian. Reducing speed. I have a partial silhouette, a damaged communications array, and a pressure reading that is fluctuating across three compartments.

[voice:BRIAN]
Any distress call?

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
None transmitted. But there is a signal. Very faint. It repeats every forty seconds.

[voice:BRIAN]
Play it.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
[emphasis]Three knocks. A pause. Two knocks.[/emphasis] No words.

[voice:BRIAN]
That sounds deliberate.

[voice:TIRZAH]
It is. Listen to the pause. Whoever made that pattern is waiting for somebody to answer.

[voice:BRIAN]
How can you tell?

[voice:TIRZAH]
Because the last two knocks are softer. They're not testing equipment. They're running out of strength.

[voice:BRIAN]
Then we don't waste time. But we don't rush in blind either.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Good. Because I have one condition.

[voice:BRIAN]
Only one? That's new.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Nobody goes over there alone.

[voice:BRIAN]
Agreed.

[voice:NARRATOR]
A quiet chime sounded from the console beside Brian. A second screen illuminated with a familiar symbol: a small green circle surrounded by a lattice of lines.

[voice:RAZACI]
I heard the word "alone," and I would like it entered into the permanent ship regulations that this is an unacceptable strategy.

[voice:BRIAN]
Razaci. Welcome to the bridge. What have you found?

[voice:RAZACI]
A puzzle. The station's external transmitters are dead, but its internal network is active. Something has isolated the communications system from the rest of the station.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Something, or someone?

[voice:RAZACI]
I cannot determine that yet. And I will not pretend otherwise.

[voice:BRIAN]
That's why I trust your reports. Tell me what you need.

[voice:RAZACI]
A clean connection. No remote takeover, no aggressive scan. If someone is hiding behind those walls, I don't want our first introduction to feel like an invasion.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Then we knock back.

[voice:BRIAN]
Green Dragon, can you transmit the same pattern through the hull?

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
Yes. Three knocks. A pause. Two knocks. Sending now.

[voice:NARRATOR]
The station remained dark. Then a single light blinked in a window near the docking ring.

[voice:TIRZAH]
There. Someone answered.

[voice:BRIAN]
All right. Let's assemble a team.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
I recommend a small boarding group. The station's structure is stable enough for entry, but not stable enough for a crowd.

[voice:BRIAN]
You stay with the ship and watch every pressure change. Razaci, guide us through the network. Tirzah, you're with me.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Naturally.

[voice:BRIAN]
That wasn't an order.

[voice:TIRZAH]
I know. I was going anyway.

[voice:NARRATOR]
Two smaller figures appeared at the bridge entrance. The dragon twins had arrived in mismatched utility suits. One carried a coil of cable. The other held a battered toolbox.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
We heard "assemble a team." For the record, we assembled ourselves.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
And for the other record, he put his helmet on backward.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
I was checking the rear visibility.

[voice:BRIAN]
Stop right there. This isn't a sightseeing trip. There may be people in danger.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
We know.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
That's why we brought tools instead of snacks.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
I brought one snack.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
You brought an entire loaf of bread.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
Emergency bread.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Enough. Can you two operate a manual airlock if the station loses power?

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
Yes. I know the release sequence and the pressure limits.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
And I can hold the outer mechanism in place while he works. We practiced on the maintenance hatch yesterday.

[voice:BRIAN]
You practiced together?

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
Three times. The first time was terrible.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
The second time was educational.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
The third time worked.

[voice:BRIAN]
Then you have a job. You handle the lock and the safety tether. If I say retreat, we retreat. No arguments.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
Understood.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
Understood.

[voice:NARRATOR]
The boarding craft crossed the short distance between ship and station. No one spoke during the final approach. The docking clamps made contact with a dull metallic thud.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
Connection secured. I am monitoring you from here. Your suits are linked to my medical and environmental systems.

[voice:BRIAN]
Thanks, Dragon.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
Bring everyone back.

[voice:BRIAN]
That's the plan.

[voice:RAZACI]
I have a map of the first corridor. It is incomplete. The central chamber is missing from every station diagram I can access.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Missing how?

[voice:RAZACI]
Not erased. Never drawn. The walls around it exist in the structural plans, but the room itself has no label, no entrance, and no listed purpose.

[voice:BRIAN]
Yet somebody is knocking from inside this place.

[voice:RAZACI]
Exactly.

[voice:NARRATOR]
The outer hatch opened. A thin current of stale air moved past the boarding team. The corridor beyond was lined with emergency lights, each one pulsing a tired amber.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Brian, look at the floor.

[voice:BRIAN]
Footprints. Fresh enough to disturb the dust.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Two sets. One person was dragging a foot. The other was helping.

[voice:BRIAN]
Twins, secure the hatch. Keep our exit open.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
On it. Tether anchored.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
Manual release is ready. And before anyone asks, yes, my helmet is facing forward.

[voice:TIRZAH]
We're proud of you.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
I choose to believe that was sincere.

[voice:RAZACI]
I am receiving a new signal from the central chamber. It is not a distress call.

[voice:BRIAN]
What is it?

[voice:RAZACI]
A request for identification. The station wants to know who we are.

[voice:BRIAN]
Then tell it.

[voice:RAZACI]
Which of us?

[voice:BRIAN]
All of us.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Good answer.

[voice:BRIAN]
Tell it we're the crew of the USS Dragon. We're here because somebody asked for help. We won't force our way into anyone's home, but we won't abandon people in trouble either.

[voice:RAZACI]
Transmitting.

[voice:NARRATOR]
A door at the end of the corridor unlocked with a heavy click. On the wall above it, a line of pale letters flickered into view.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
I can see the message through your helmet cameras. It reads: [emphasis]ONE MAY ENTER. THE OTHERS MUST REMAIN.[/emphasis]

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
That's a very rude welcome sign.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
And a very obvious trap.

[voice:TIRZAH]
We agreed. Nobody alone.

[voice:BRIAN]
Razaci, can the door hear us?

[voice:RAZACI]
Yes.

[voice:BRIAN]
Then here's our answer. [emphasis]We came as a team. We enter as a team, or we don't enter at all.[/emphasis]

[voice:NARRATOR]
For several seconds, nothing happened. Then the letters vanished. The door opened wider, revealing a second corridor and, far beyond it, the unmistakable sound of a human voice.

[voice:UNKNOWN VOICE]
You answered the knocking.

[voice:BRIAN]
Yes. We're here to help.

[voice:UNKNOWN VOICE]
Then please hurry. The others are still inside.

[voice:TIRZAH]
How many others?

[voice:UNKNOWN VOICE]
I don't know anymore.

[voice:RAZACI]
Brian, the station's power is dropping. We have limited time.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
I am preparing emergency power transfer. Twins, I may need you at the external junction.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
We're ready.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
Together.

[voice:BRIAN]
Good. Everybody knows their part. We find those people, we restore the station, and we come home.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Together.

[voice:RAZACI]
Together.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
Together.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
And afterward, emergency bread.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
Afterward.

[voice:NARRATOR]
The crew moved toward the voice. Behind them, the door remained open. For the first time since they had arrived, the silent station seemed willing to let them in.

CHAPTER TWO — THE 1996 ASSIGNMENTS
Audio Studio full-cast edition

[voice:NARRATOR]
The signal from the silent station resolved into a calendar. The year was 1996. A row of office computers flickered across the USS Dragon's forward display. This was not a new distress call. It was a record of work already done.

[voice:BRIAN]
Hold the ship steady. Before we go chasing another mysterious hallway, tell me what we're looking at.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
A memory archive. Sacramento, California. The records are incomplete, but the dates agree. Someone was working with computers long before the later injuries and recoveries we've seen.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Then we don't rush it. We read what is here, and we don't fill the blanks with guesses.

[voice:RAZACI]
Agreed. There are at least two separate work assignments in this archive. Someone merged them into a single folder. That is why the timeline looks wrong.

[voice:BRIAN]
Two assignments? Show me the first one.

[voice:RAZACI]
A staffing or headhunting agency handled the candidate's references, study, research, paperwork, testing, and placement. The agency's name is not preserved in this record.

[voice:TIRZAH]
So a person didn't simply walk into a building and become the computer expert. There was preparation, evaluation, and somebody deciding that the person could do the work.

[voice:BRIAN]
That's what I want the crew to notice. Real work has steps. You don't skip the steps just because the ending sounds exciting.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
[emphasis]Crew protocol updated: evidence before conclusions.[/emphasis]

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
I would like the record to show that I have always supported evidence.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
You once submitted a sandwich as evidence of a coolant leak.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
It was wet.

[voice:TIRZAH]
And it was your sandwich.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
An important distinction that the investigation refused to respect.

[voice:BRIAN]
Enough. Razaci, assignment one.

[voice:RAZACI]
The first placement concerns updating computers at McClellan Air Force Base, in the Sacramento area. The worker was tasked with repetitive machine updates. The surviving account says he wrote automation to perform much of that repeated work.

[voice:BRIAN]
Show me the process, not a dramatic reconstruction.

[voice:RAZACI]
On screen: a sequence of routine updates, machine after machine. The precise original code is not available. I will not invent it.

[voice:TIRZAH]
That's important. We know what the automation accomplished from the account, but not every line of the program.

[voice:BRIAN]
Still, the idea is familiar. If you have to repeat the same safe operation over and over, you start looking for a way to make the machine carry the repetition. Then the person can concentrate on the exceptions.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
An efficient division of labor. The operator supplies judgment. The system supplies consistency.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
Unless the system is my brother.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
I supply consistency! I am consistently magnificent.

[voice:TIRZAH]
And consistently interrupting.

[voice:BRIAN]
Tirzah, I want you on the chronology. Razaci, separate the sources. Green Dragon, check whether the archive has confused one job with another. Twins, you get a real assignment: compare the dates on every record and flag anything that doesn't fit.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
An actual assignment? With responsibility?

[voice:BRIAN]
Yes. You asked to be treated like crew. This is what crew does.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
We'll do it. No jokes until we've checked the dates.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
I reserve the right to make one extremely small joke if a date is shaped funny.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
Denied.

[voice:NARRATOR]
For a while, only the soft click of archive pages filled the bridge. The twins worked side by side, tracing dates rather than chasing distractions. Tirzah assembled a timeline. Razaci split the records into two folders.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Brian, look here. The base assignment is one thread. But there's another, also in 1996, involving MCI, Packard Bell, and NetDay '96. It needs its own place in the history.

[voice:BRIAN]
Good catch. What do we actually know about that second thread?

[voice:RAZACI]
That it was a separate Sacramento-area staffing assignment connected to MCI, Packard Bell, and NetDay '96. We should not pretend we have a complete job description when we don't.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
I have found no evidence that these were the same employer, the same site, or the same task. They will remain separate.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
Date check complete. Two folders were incorrectly combined. We have marked the uncertain entries rather than forcing them into an order.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
And I did not make a single joke.

[voice:TIRZAH]
You look physically unwell.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
I can hear a pun screaming inside my skull.

[voice:BRIAN]
You did good work. Both of you.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
Thank you. That means more than you think.

[voice:RAZACI]
The reconstruction is becoming clearer. This person worked through a staffing agency, took on technical assignments, and learned to make computers do repetitive work. Later archives show teaching and program-building. There may be a connection in skills, but we should not assume every event followed directly from the one before it.

[voice:TIRZAH]
There is something else. The way this person describes the work isn't about showing off. It's about figuring out how a task works, then making it work better.

[voice:BRIAN]
That's how you learn. You touch the problem. You test something. You find out what breaks. You fix it. And then somebody hands you a harder problem.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
Brian, your description closely matches the archive's operating philosophy.

[voice:BRIAN]
Maybe it matches a lot of people who build things.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Maybe. We don't have to decide today.

[voice:RAZACI]
There is one more detail in the recovered testimony. During that Sacramento period, the worker was staying with his girlfriend and her family. The work happened within an ordinary human life, not in isolation.

[voice:TIRZAH]
That matters. A résumé can make a life look like a row of job titles. But somebody was living somewhere, coming home, eating dinner, trying to figure out the next day.

[voice:BRIAN]
And learning while the world kept moving. Keep that detail. Don't turn the person into a machine just because the records are about machines.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
I have a question, and it's a serious one.

[voice:BRIAN]
Go ahead.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
If the code did the repetitive work, did the person get more time to think about other things?

[voice:BRIAN]
That's the hope. Automation should give you room to do the parts that need a mind.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
Then I understand why this archive matters. It isn't just about one program. It's about a habit of solving problems.

[voice:RAZACI]
And habits can survive when other memories become difficult to reach.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Careful. That's a possibility, not a conclusion about this person's life.

[voice:RAZACI]
Correction accepted. The archive contains later accounts of injury and rehabilitation, but we must not infer a specific psychological mechanism from these records.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
[emphasis]Timeline stabilized.[/emphasis] The Sacramento assignments are now separate, labeled, and preserved with their uncertainties intact.

[voice:BRIAN]
Then we've done something useful today. Not a dramatic rescue. Not a battle. We listened to the evidence and worked together.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
I would still like a medal.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
For reading dates?

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
For heroic restraint. I was hilarious in my head for forty-seven straight minutes.

[voice:TIRZAH]
That may be the bravest thing you've ever done.

[voice:BRIAN]
All right, crew. Save the two records. Put the unanswered questions where we can see them. We aren't racing to a conclusion about whose life this is.

[voice:RAZACI]
Saved. And I found the next line of testimony. It describes someone standing in front of students, trying to teach them what a computer could do.

[voice:TIRZAH]
We have seen a classroom before. Maybe this time we can learn what happened inside it, not just what was written on the wall.

[voice:BRIAN]
Then that's where we go next. Together.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
Course set. No one left behind. No record silently rewritten.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
And no sandwich-based investigations.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
I make no promises.

[voice:NARRATOR]
The two 1996 folders settled into the archive, side by side but no longer confused. Ahead waited a classroom—and a story about the people who learned there.

END OF CHAPTER TWO

CHAPTER THREE — THE PEOPLE BEHIND THE DESKS
Audio Studio full-cast edition

[voice:NARRATOR]
The USS Dragon held position above the archive. On the bridge, the two Sacramento folders remained open. A third record waited beside them: EDUCATION — INSTRUCTOR.

[voice:BRIAN]
Before anybody opens that, I want to know what we actually found yesterday.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
Two assignments, not one. A staffing agency. Computer updates at McClellan. A separate MCI, Packard Bell, NetDay '96 thread. And one hero who did not make a single joke for almost an hour.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
Three facts and one completely unnecessary autobiography.

[voice:BRIAN]
Good. You remembered the distinction. Now tell me what we don't know.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
The original program code. The full duties of the other assignment. And how those jobs connect to the classroom.

[voice:TIRZAH]
That's the answer I wanted to hear. We're not here to make the records sound prettier. We're here to understand the person.

[voice:RAZACI]
The next archive has two layers. One concerns the subject's own education after a serious injury. The other concerns teaching at a university. We have evidence of both, but the records don't give us a complete calendar for every event.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
I recommend a controlled review. No simulated voices until we establish which statements are testimony and which are reconstruction.

[voice:BRIAN]
Agreed. Nobody puts words in the mouth of a student we can't identify.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
Can I put words in my brother's mouth?

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
Try it and you'll be eating your own helmet.

[voice:TIRZAH]
See? Natural talent. Neither of you needs help.

[voice:RAZACI]
Opening the education record now.

[voice:NARRATOR]
A classroom appeared on the forward screen. The chairs were empty. Beside the teacher's desk stood a rolling cart with a computer, a tangle of cables, and a whiteboard covered in half-erased instructions.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
This is a visual reconstruction, not a verified photograph.

[voice:BRIAN]
Thank you. Keep saying that when it matters. Now, what's the lesson?

[voice:RAZACI]
The lesson title was not preserved. The record does establish that the subject later taught at a university.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Then let's investigate what teaching demanded of him. Not just what title he held.

[voice:BRIAN]
Teaching demands that you explain something you think you understand. Then somebody asks one question and proves you skipped six steps.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
I would be an excellent student.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
You once asked whether a keyboard could catch a cold.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
It had a virus!

[voice:BRIAN]
And that, somehow, is a useful example. If a student says something strange, don't just laugh. Find out what they mean.

[voice:TIRZAH]
So you wouldn't start by showing off what you know?

[voice:BRIAN]
No. I'd start by finding out what they can do. If they can open a program but can't save a file, you teach saving a file. If they can save a file but can't find it again, you teach folders. You build from the part they actually have.

[voice:RAZACI]
The method you describe is consistent with practical instruction, but I cannot verify it was the precise method used in the historical classroom.

[voice:BRIAN]
Then label it as our discussion. Don't sneak it into the record.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
[emphasis]Evidence boundary maintained.[/emphasis]

[voice:TIRZAH]
Razaci, bring up the education timeline before the teaching record.

[voice:RAZACI]
The account describes a GED, then an associate's degree, a bachelor's degree, and a master's degree in psychology completed in 2006. There is also later doctoral coursework and technical study. Exact dates for every credential are not established here.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
That is a lot of classrooms for one person.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
I went to a classroom once.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
You were delivering a pizza.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
And I learned the door was locked. Education!

[voice:TIRZAH]
There's something extraordinary about the sequence, but I don't want to make it into a slogan. The same archive contains a motorcycle crash, severe injuries, and a long recovery.

[voice:BRIAN]
Then don't call it a miracle and move on. Ask what the work looked like. Studying while you're rebuilding your daily life isn't one glorious moment. It's a lot of ordinary days.

[voice:RAZACI]
I can retrieve the earlier rehabilitation records without replaying the entire accident.

[voice:BRIAN]
Do that. Just enough to connect them.

[voice:RAZACI]
The archive reports a period using a wheelchair, then a walker, then practice in a swimming pool. It also describes memory difficulties in ordinary tasks, followed by a simulated work assessment.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
So learning wasn't separate from recovery. It was part of how the person continued living.

[voice:TIRZAH]
And the classroom might have been a place where that person could give something back.

[voice:BRIAN]
Might have. That's a good question, not an established motive.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
I have another question.

[voice:BRIAN]
I'm listening.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
If the teacher forgot something while teaching, what happens?

[voice:BRIAN]
You tell the class you need to check. You don't invent an answer because you're standing at the front of the room.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
That sounds suspiciously like our archive rules.

[voice:BRIAN]
Exactly. A classroom and a ship both get dangerous when somebody's too proud to say, 'I don't know.'

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
I will enter that into crew procedure. [emphasis]Uncertainty is a reportable fact, not a failure.[/emphasis]

[voice:RAZACI]
A new document has appeared. It is not a student transcript. It is a list of technical credentials: MSP, A Plus, and C I W.

[voice:TIRZAH]
We saw some of those letters in the earlier classroom archive. Can we determine which credential came first?

[voice:RAZACI]
Not reliably from the available evidence.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
I propose we arrange them by which one looks best on a cape.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
I propose you stop touching the evidence.

[voice:BRIAN]
Both proposals received. Only one approved.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
The cape?

[voice:BRIAN]
The stopping.

[voice:NARRATOR]
A second display illuminated. This one showed no classroom at all. It showed a small desk with a stack of notebooks, a computer monitor, and an unfinished page.

[voice:TIRZAH]
That doesn't look like the university.

[voice:RAZACI]
It is a symbolic workspace generated from the record's themes. The archive does not preserve the room's exact appearance.

[voice:BRIAN]
Then what does it tell us?

[voice:RAZACI]
The subject continued technical and academic study across different stages of life. The records also mention completing a second quarter of doctoral classes.

[voice:TIRZAH]
That's a person who kept returning to the work.

[voice:BRIAN]
Or someone who had reasons we haven't heard yet. We should meet those reasons before we decide what they mean.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
Then how do we investigate a classroom when we don't have the students' words?

We investigate what we can. The tools. The responsibilities. The work of explaining. And we ask what evidence is missing.

[voice:TIRZAH]
I can build a question sheet: What subjects were taught? What materials survive? What did the students practice? Which memories are firsthand?

[voice:RAZACI]
I will mark every answer as verified, user-reported, inferred, or fictional reconstruction. No category will silently become another.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
I will keep the original records untouched. We can make a working copy for the crew.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
I'll compare the technical terms across the folders. If the same phrase appears in the Sacramento assignment and the teaching records, I'll flag it without claiming they happened together.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
And I will test whether a complete beginner can follow the instructions.

[voice:BRIAN]
That's actually a very good job for you.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
I have trained for this my entire life.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
By refusing to read instructions?

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
By being the world's most advanced beginner.

[voice:TIRZAH]
Brian, watch him. If he can't understand a step, that's a clue about the step.

[voice:BRIAN]
All right. Twin One, pretend you've never seen this machine. What do you do first?

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
I introduce myself politely. Hello, machine. I'm available for management.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
The machine declines.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
Rude.

[voice:BRIAN]
Now try the power button.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN ONE]
That tiny thing? Why didn't anyone label it 'BEGIN YOUR DESTINY'?

[voice:TIRZAH]
Because somebody would press it during an emergency.

[voice:DRAGON TWIN TWO]
Somebody already did. He was my brother.

[voice:RAZACI]
The exercise is revealing a useful distinction. Instructions written for an expert often omit steps a beginner needs.

[voice:BRIAN]
There. That's a real lesson. Not a lecture. We watched a problem happen and learned from it.

[voice:TIRZAH]
And everybody helped. Even the machine that refused management.

[voice:GREEN DRAGON]
My refusal stands.

[voice:NARRATOR]
